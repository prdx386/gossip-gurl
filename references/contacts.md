# Resolving people at the end client

The goal is a short list of real roles with named holders, each with a source and a date, so the user can pick an entry point. Quality beats quantity: three verified people in the right layers are worth more than fifteen names from a directory.

## Source hierarchy

Use sources in this order; a lower source confirms, it does not override a higher one.

1. The company's own pages: Impressum, management page, press releases, contact pages, data-protection notice. These give executives, portfolios, tenure, and the email pattern.
2. The company's job ads for security or IT roles. They name teams ("OT-Sicherheitsteam"), reporting lines ("unterstützt die OT-Sicherheitsbeauftragte"), locations and vacancies. A vacant leadership role explains why externals are being bought.
3. Annual and sustainability reports, section on information security or cybersecurity. Groups describe their governance there: who the CISO reports to, how segments are organised.
4. Conference and panel speaker lists (sector conferences, industry associations, trade press events). Bios give current title and employer with a date.
5. Org-chart aggregators and people directories (theorg, rocketreach, contactout). Useful for titles, unreliable for currency and for email formats. Never take an email format from them.
6. LinkedIn through search snippets. Search `"<company>" <role term> LinkedIn`; the snippet shows name, headline and recent activity. Profile pages are not fetched and not scraped.

Queries are built from the organisation name and role terms. A person's name is searched only to confirm their current role at that organisation (a conference bio, a press release), never to collect anything beyond it. Nothing from the user's profile goes into a query.

## Layers to fill

- Sponsor: executive with security or IT in the portfolio. Note since when. A new executive commissioning an independent assessment or a new CISO building external capacity are common triggers for postings like these.
- Owner: CISO, ISB, Head of Information Security, OT security lead, Head of Cyber Defense. Adjust the title list to the sector.
- Operational: team leads who will be interview partners, only if they appear publicly.
- Group layer for subsidiaries: group CISO, CIO, CSO. Groups often centralise procurement and governance; the posting's "central vs. decentral" language points here.

## Dating and currency

Every source carries a date in the report. Roles change: a speaker bio from eighteen months ago is a lead to verify, not a fact. When two sources disagree, prefer the newer one and say so.

## Email patterns

Derive the pattern only from the company's own published addresses (sales contacts, press, data protection, redaktion). Note the rule for umlauts and double names. Present the derived address as "very likely" and say it is unverified.

Recommend the channel in this order: a message on a professional network, a call through the published switchboard, or the broker. Say why when the user is in Germany: a cold email to a business contact needs prior consent under § 7 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UWG, and a derived address at a regulated company tends to land in a phishing queue anyway. If the user emails regardless, that is their call; the draft then names where the user found the person's role (Art. 14 GDPR expects it, and it is the honest opener).

## What not to do

- No home addresses, phone numbers beyond published business lines, family or private social media.
- No bulk harvesting of staff lists.
- No contact details for people who are not decision makers or interview partners for this request.
- No research results on people kept beyond the approach: the report is the user's, the skill stores roles and sources only in a deep-mode case file and reminds the user to delete it when the case closes.
