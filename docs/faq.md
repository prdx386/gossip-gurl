# FAQ

**Is this legal?**
Looking up a company's management and a decision maker's professional role from public sources is ordinary business research, and the GDPR still applies to you as the person processing that data: `SECURITY.md` section 4 says what that means in practice (legitimate interest and its balancing test, data minimisation, telling people where you got their details, honouring objections). The skill stays on the business side of that line by design; staying there in your outreach is your job. General information, not legal advice.

**Can I just email the person?**
In Germany, a cold business email needs prior consent (§ 7 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UWG), also for a single personal message. The skill therefore recommends a professional-network message, the published switchboard, or the broker, and says so in the report. If you email anyway, the draft names where you saw the person's role, which the GDPR expects at first contact. Your decision, your risk.

**I got the posting from a broker. Can I go to the client directly?**
Broker and platform terms usually contain non-circumvention clauses. The skill's drafts to end clients are for positioning ("does this topic sit with you, would you like to talk?"), not for pitching the broker's request past the broker. Apply through the broker if you apply at all. Read your platform's terms.

**Why does it say "unverified" next to every email address?**
Because it derives the pattern from the company's published addresses and never checks whether a specific mailbox exists. Checking would mean probing a mail server, which the skill does not do.

**It named a company with 90 %. Can I rely on that?**
90 % means one mirrored posting described the client in a way that fits one organisation, and nothing contradicted it. The recruiter can still surprise you. Treat it as a strong lead, write to the person with a professional hook rather than "I saw your posting", and let the recruiter confirm.

**Why not just ask the recruiter?**
Do that too. The skill tells you when that is the faster route (exclusive mandates with a single broker). Its value is that you talk to the right person at the client while the recruiter is still collecting CVs, and that you know before the call whether the formal hurdles rule you out.

**Does it send anything?**
No. It drafts in the conversation. It does not create drafts in your mail client either. You send.

**Does my CV end up in search queries?**
No. The skill is instructed to build queries from the posting text, organisation names and role terms only. Your profile appears in the report and in drafts you review, nowhere else.

**Can it read my LinkedIn?**
No, and it does not fetch LinkedIn pages at all, neither profiles nor job ads. It uses search-engine snippets of public pages, which is what any web search shows.

**Why does it ask me to confirm every self-rating?**
A "rate yourself 1 to 10 per requirement" table is your statement to the client. The skill proposes values from your project list and shows the evidence; you confirm or correct. An inflated 9 you cannot defend in the interview costs more than an honest 6.

**How much does a run cost?**
Standard mode is about 30 tool calls (web searches, page fetches, file reads) with a cap per stage; when a stage's cap is spent the report says what was not opened. Deep mode three to five times that. The skill tells you before going deep.

**Does it work outside Germany?**
The process does. The experience tables (which platforms leak what, how client families buy) are DACH. Contributions for other markets are welcome; see CONTRIBUTING.md.

**Does it work outside IT security?**
Yes. The process is sector-neutral. The examples are security because that is where it was built; the procurement-signature table grows with the cases people report.

**The parser says `freelancers_excluded: false` but the posting excludes freelancers.**
The parser is a heuristic over phrasings it has seen; `false` means "not recognised", not "not required". The skill reads the posting itself. Report the phrasing as an issue and it gets added.

**Why "gossip-gurl"?**
Because it finds out who is behind an anonymous posting and tells you, with sources. "Spotted:" is the first line of every report.
